Capability Snapshot · Worked Example

A resume tells you where a nurse has been. Not what they can run.

Your ICU posting asks for advanced cardiac, neuro and hemodynamic support, charge responsibilities, and evidence-based care planning. Every applicant resume will claim ICU experience. Almost none will tell you which of those five things they can actually do.

So somebody reads the stack and infers it. That inference is the most clinically consequential judgement in the whole process, and it is made from the least reliable document in the file.

What the posting actually asks for

Twelve requirements, sitting in prose.

FaujX reads the posting and pulls the requirements out of it — separating the hard gates you cannot waive from the capabilities you weigh. That separation is the part resume screening usually loses.

Hard gates — pass or fail

Arizona RN licence. Not negotiable, not scorable. Either the nurse can practise in Arizona on day one, or the rest of the file is academic.

BLS and ACLS. Current, per department guidelines.

Nursing degree, accredited school. BSN preferred, not required.

Minimum one year ICU or acute care.

Capabilities — weighed, not gated

Bedside care for critically ill patients · advanced cardiac support · neuro assessment and support · hemodynamic monitoring · care planning via the nursing process · evidence-based practice · clinical judgement · supervision and delegation · interdisciplinary communication · performance under pressure.

Ten of them. Each one gets its own line, its own evidence, and its own gap.

Why the split matters. A single blended percentage lets a licensure problem hide behind a strong device history. Gates are checked first and reported separately. Nothing about a candidate's ECMO experience can raise a score on a licence they do not hold.
Gate check

What the resume can prove, and what it cannot.

This is the part most screening tools quietly skip. A resume is a claim, not a credential. Where the document does not carry the evidence, the report says so rather than inferring it.

The part the headline number hides

93 and 80 are not the same as first and second.

The percentages point at Marcus. Read the posting again and it is less obvious than that.

What the score rewards

Marcus wins decisively on the technical spine of the role — ECMO, IABP, Impella, CRRT, neuro ICU, CCRN. If the unit's constraint is device competence at the bedside, that is the file.

He is also the candidate with no evidenced charge or preceptor experience, on a posting that explicitly asks for supervision, delegation, and taking charge when needed. That score is an inference, not a finding.

What the score under-weights

Renata is charge nurse, lead preceptor to fourteen new graduates, Practice Council chair. On the leadership line of your posting she is the stronger of the two, and it is not close.

Her gap is hemodynamic depth. That gap is trainable on a unit with a preceptor pipeline. Charge-level judgement across nine years is not.

This is the argument the report exists to start, not to settle. Which of those two things your unit is short of right now is a question about your census, your staffing mix and your bench — and it lives with you, not with a system. What the snapshot does is put both candidates in front of you as structured evidence in a few minutes, so the argument happens before the interviews rather than after them.
Boundaries

What this does not do.

Worth stating plainly, because the category has an honesty problem.

Does not
  • Reject, rank-order or auto-advance anyone
  • Verify licensure — that is a primary-source check
  • Score compassion, empathy or bedside manner from a document
  • Analyse face or voice. Assessment captures screen and audio only
  • Replace your judgement, your recruiters, or your ATS
Does
  • Derive the requirements from the posting you wrote
  • Evaluate every applicant against the same criteria in the same order
  • Show the evidence line behind every score, and label inferences as inferences
  • Separate hard gates from weighted capabilities
  • Hand you the questions worth asking, per candidate
