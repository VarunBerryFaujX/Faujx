Judged on What You Can Do: A Candidate's Guide to Fair AI Screening

What you are right to expect from AI screening, how FaujX works for you, and how to get the most out of it.

October 2026  ·  FaujX, a product of Arber, Inc.

AI screening is now a normal part of applying for jobs. You have every right to expect it to be fair, open, and checked by people.

01The short version

A good AI screening should tell you up front what it is, show you the same results the employer sees, judge you on what you can actually do, and leave the hiring decision to people who have met you.

That is how FaujX is built. This guide explains what to expect and how to make the process work in your favor.

03Why AI screening is here to stay, and how it can work for you

Job seekers once moved from paper resumes to online job portals. Structured AI screening is the next step, and stepping away from it usually means missing the opportunity.

Done well, it can work in your favor:

  • Your application actually gets looked at. A popular role can attract hundreds of applications, and people reviewing by hand often stop before the end of the pile. A good system measures every applicant, first or five-hundredth, against the same standard.
  • You are more than your resume. A resume lists what you claim. A capability assessment lets you show what you can do.
  • Your school or past employer should not decide your chances. Ratings built on evidence rather than pedigree give a fair shot to people from non-traditional backgrounds.

04What you are right to expect from any AI screening

Whichever company or tool you encounter, these are fair questions to ask:

  1. Was I told an AI is involved, and what it evaluates? In some places this is required. New York City requires employers to notify candidates before using these tools, and Illinois requires employers to tell applicants when AI is used in hiring decisions.
  2. Will I see my results?
  3. Is a person making the decision?
  4. Has the tool been checked for bias? In New York City, employers must commission a yearly independent bias audit and publish a summary.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by location and are changing quickly.

06A straight answer on using AI during the assessment

Using AI to prepare is fine: researching the company, practicing answers, getting feedback on your resume. Using AI to answer for you during the assessment is not.

This happens often. In one 2026 survey of 1,500 US respondents, 26.9% said they had used AI during a live interview (GCheck). Employers know this, and they are watching for it.

FaujX verifies that candidates are who they say they are, and detects plagiarism and AI assistance. No detection is perfect, and we say so openly. But there is a simpler reason to do your own work: the assessment exists to show what you can do. If AI answers for you, the hiring team meets someone different in the live interview, and that is where it falls apart.

07How to get the most out of it

  1. Read the declaration. Know what is being assessed before you begin.
  2. Show your thinking, not just the answer. Ratings are built on evidence, so explain how you got there.
  3. Do your own work. It is the version of you the hiring team will meet in the interview.
  4. Read your reports. If you move forward, use them to prepare for the interview. If not, use them to understand your gaps.
  5. Ask questions. If anything is unclear, ask the hiring company. It is fair to want to understand how you are being assessed.
