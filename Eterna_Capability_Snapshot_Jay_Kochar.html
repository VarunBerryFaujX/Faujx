What the shapes mean

Two of these are worth an hour. Two aren't — yet.

Evidence supports interview time

Colin Marsh  90%

Reads mid-tier on paper

Non-CS degree, no certifications, mid-level title, 45-person employer. On a stack of 140 he is easy to skim past.

What the résumé actually carries: latency work with a stated method and a measured before and after, a large zero-downtime schema migration, an on-call rotation and alerting he built from nothing, an authorisation rebuild carried through a security audit, and two engineers he mentors. Four of those years are in policy and claims data.

Evidence supports interview time

Nadia Farooqi  89%

Reads strongest on paper

Eight years, senior title, Carnegie Mellon, three cloud certifications. Every keyword in your posting appears somewhere on the page.

Coverage is genuinely broad. What thins out is ownership: much of the strongest-sounding material is written as participation — contributed to, involved in, supported, exposure to — rather than as work she led and measured. Worth the hour, and worth spending it on what she personally decided.

Not yet — evidence is thin, not absent

Marcus Feld  68%

Reads interesting, reads risky

The widest band of half-filled squares in the set. Real REST and cloud work, real client-facing skill, seven years of engineering discipline before the switch into software.

But nine short consulting engagements across six stacks means nothing was owned long enough to show depth, and observability and GraphQL are simply not on the page. This is the profile where a résumé cannot answer the question either way — the shape says assess him, not pass on him.

Not yet — nine capabilities unevidenced

Hana Yoshida  37%

Reads impressive on paper

M.S. Computer Science, benchmark numbers, database internals, Rust. On most screens this résumé advances.

Against this posting, nine of fourteen capabilities have no evidence at all: no microservices, no REST, no GraphQL, no cloud provisioning, no secure coding, no authorisation. She is deep and she is narrow, and the narrow part is most of your job description.

What your team walks in with

Questions built from this stack, not from a question bank

Every question below is anchored to something specific in a specific résumé. Half of them press on what's missing. Half press on what's claimed — because a strength that can't be explained under questioning was never a strength.
