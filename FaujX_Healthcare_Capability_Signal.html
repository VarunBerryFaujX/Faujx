If the assumption was that this only works for software engineers, this is the test that settles it. One critical care nursing requisition. Two candidates. The resume layer and the capability layer disagree completely.

The role is not the input. The role's derived capabilities are. That is why the domain does not matter.

Registered Nurse · Intensive Care Unit · 13-week contract · Bethesda, MD
The test

Built as a controlled experiment, not a demo.

A demo shows you the system agreeing with you. A controlled test tells you something you did not already know. The two candidates were deliberately constructed so that a conventional resume screen and a capability assessment would reach opposite conclusions.

The requisition

A single ICU role — not a multi-specialty agency posting. 24-bed adult medical/surgical ICU. 1:2 assignment. CRRT on the unit. Independent practice from week two.

What was derived

14 capabilities pulled from the requisition's stated responsibilities and minimum qualifications. The same 14 applied identically to both candidates.

What was withheld

No hints, no ranking, no context about which candidate was intended to win. Two resumes and one job description. Nothing else.

What the hiring team receives

The gaps become the interview.

Each candidate's open capabilities are converted into probe questions for the client's own interviewers. The system does not ask them. It tells the humans who will what is worth asking, and why.

Ventilator management gap

The job requires independent management of ventilated patients. Can you describe your most significant experience managing a patient on a mechanical ventilator, including your role in weaning trials and extubation readiness assessments?

Vasoactive management gap

This role involves titrating vasoactive infusions like norepinephrine and vasopressin. Walk me through a time you managed a patient on multiple vasoactive drips — your assessment, your titration decisions, and how you monitored for effectiveness and side effects.

CRRT experience gap

Our ICU frequently initiates and monitors CRRT. What is your experience with it, including initiating the circuit, troubleshooting common issues, and managing fluid balance for these patients?

Prioritization & workload

As a charge nurse you've managed staffing and patient flow. How do you approach prioritizing competing demands with a 1:2 assignment, especially when one patient is unstable or requires CRRT or post-arrest care?

The questions are different for each candidate, because the gaps are different. A strong candidate gets depth probes on demonstrated strengths. A weaker one gets direct tests of the specific things the evidence did not support.

Scope

What this does not do.

In clinical hiring the limits matter more than the capabilities, and they should come from us rather than from your compliance team three weeks in.

Stated plainly

  • It does not verify credentials. Licence status, certification currency, and employment history are primary-source checks against the state board and the employer. Self-reported credentials are treated as unverified until your team confirms them.
  • It does not assess hands-on clinical skill. Whether a nurse can place a line, manage a circuit at the bedside, or run a code is psychomotor competency. No document-based or screen-based assessment reaches it, and none should claim to.
  • It does not decide. No candidate is advanced, rejected, or ranked into a hiring outcome by this system. It produces a signal and the evidence behind it. The decision, the interview, and the offer stay entirely with your team.
  • It does not replace your recruiters. It removes the part of their day spent establishing whether a resume is worth reading, and returns the part spent on judgment.
  • It captures no biometric data. No facial analysis, no voice analysis, no emotion inference — relevant to NYC Local Law 144, the Illinois AI hiring statute, and equivalent frameworks.

Method

How to read this honestly.

Both candidates are synthetic. Renata Kowalczyk-Byrne and Marcus Ovadia do not exist. The employers, schools, phone numbers, and email addresses are fictional. They were written specifically to construct a case where the resume layer and the capability layer disagree, so that the disagreement could be observed rather than asserted. No real nurse's record was processed, and no real candidate data appears anywhere in this document.

What was measured. The 14 capabilities were derived from the requisition text — its responsibilities, minimum qualifications, and preferred qualifications — and applied identically to both candidates. The verdicts and evidence shown are the system's own output, unedited. What was not measured. Clinical performance, patient outcomes, hands-on competency, and credential authenticity. What this is not. A hiring decision, a clinical judgement, or a claim about either candidate's fitness to practise. It is a signal about the distance between a stated requirement and the evidence a document supports.

The interesting question isn't nursing. It's yours.

This was run on a domain the system was never tuned for, using a requisition it had never seen. If it holds for critical care nursing, the question worth answering is what it does against a live role in your own pipeline — with your requirements, your applicants, and your team reading the output.
