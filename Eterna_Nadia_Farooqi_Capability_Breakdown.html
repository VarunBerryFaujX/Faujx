The register column

Fourteen capabilities. Zero sentences where she is the actor.

Read the right-hand column down the page. Every piece of evidence on Nadia's résumé is written in a register that places her near the work rather than at it. That is not a criticism — it may simply be how she writes. But it is the one thing a résumé stage can surface and a résumé stage cannot resolve.

The 89% is honest about what it measures. She covers your stack, and that coverage is real. What it cannot tell you is how much of it was hers — and at Eterna, where an engineer owns services rather than sitting inside a three-thousand-person platform org, that distinction is the whole question.

If you take the hour

Six questions, each anchored to a specific line

Three press on what the résumé leaves unevidenced. Three press on what it claims, and all three ask for a decision she personally made.

Ownership
Criterion 01
You describe being part of the platform team behind a microservices architecture serving millions of requests. Pick one service in that architecture and take me through a design decision you personally made and defended. What was the alternative, and why did you rule it out?
On-call
Criterion 14
Observability appears on your résumé but on-call does not. Describe a production incident you personally handled. What failed, what did you do in the first ten minutes, and what changed afterwards to stop it recurring?
Security
Criteria 07 & 08
OAuth2 and OIDC are on your skills list but no authorisation work appears in your experience. Describe an authentication or authorisation model you designed or materially changed. What were the failure modes you were guarding against?
API evolution
Criterion 02
You contributed to API standards adopted across the organisation. Which specific guideline in those standards was yours, what problem was it solving, and how did you get engineers outside your team to follow it?
Performance
Criteria 05 & 06
Take one slow query or one slow endpoint you worked on. What were the numbers before, what did you change, and what were the numbers after? How did you measure it?
Domain
Cross-cutting
You would be moving into insurance. Describe a time you had to learn an unfamiliar business domain fast enough to make good technical calls in it. What did you get wrong first?
Two of these are worth asking regardless of who the candidate is. The other four exist only because of what her particular résumé does and doesn't say — which is the point of running the stage at all.

What this is not

Stage 1 doesn't decide anything

This is a résumé-stage read. It tells you Nadia is worth an hour and exactly what to spend that hour on. It does not tell you whether to hire her, and it is not built to. The questions above are the ones Stages 2 through 4 are designed to answer under structured conditions — before your engineers spend interview slots, rather than after.
